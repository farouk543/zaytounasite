Montreal, Laval, Toronto, Ottawa, Mississauga: many families from Tunisia and North Africa have built their lives in Canada. Children often do very well at school, yet parents ask the same questions: how do we keep Arabic alive, pass on the Quran and sometimes prepare for a return or an exam in our home country? Here is how to organise it from Canada without overloading the children.

The challenge for families in Canada

In Quebec, schooling is mostly in French; in Ontario and other provinces, mostly in English. Either way, Arabic fades quickly at home once children spend their days at school. Add the time difference with family back home and weekend schools that are far away or full, and many parents give up for lack of a practical solution.

The key is a small, fixed weekly appointment. Two short, well-placed sessions beat an unrealistic plan.

Three possible goals

  • Keeping Arabic: reading, writing and speaking, to stay close to family and culture.
  • Learning the Quran: correct reading, tajweed and memorisation, at a pace suited to age.
  • Following the home-country curriculum: useful for a return to Tunisia or a national exam.

Solving the time-zone puzzle

Montreal and Toronto are 6 hours behind Tunisia in winter and 5 hours in summer. The best slot is the weekend morning in Canada, which is the afternoon in Tunisia.

Making learning a family habit

  1. Choose the slot together and put it on the family calendar.
  2. Set up a quiet corner with the computer, a headset and the Arabic notebook.
  3. Revise five to ten minutes in the evening: a letter, a verse, a new word.
  4. Share progress with grandparents by video call: a wonderful motivation.

Frequently asked questions

My child speaks French or English at school. Can they still learn Arabic well?

Yes. Bilingual children often pick up a third language easily. Consistency matters more than the number of hours.

Can my child follow the Tunisian curriculum from Canada?

Yes, with regular support in the main subjects. To sit a Tunisian national exam from abroad, check the official conditions for the year with the ministry or the consulate.

What are the best times from Canada?

Saturday and Sunday mornings: it is afternoon in Tunisia and the child is well rested.
