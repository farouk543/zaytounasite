Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin in early February, depending on the sighting of the crescent moon. For many families, it is the perfect occasion to give their children a real connection with the Quran: learning to read it correctly, memorising a few surahs and understanding what they recite. That starts with choosing the right lessons. Here are the criteria that truly matter.

Why start a few weeks before Ramadan?

Starting in January leaves time to lay the foundations and build a habit. During Ramadan, children can recite what they have learned in their prayers and with the family, which greatly boosts their motivation. After Ramadan, the momentum is there: you just need to keep the rhythm.

The goal is not to memorise as much as possible before Ramadan, but to nurture a love of the Quran that lasts long after it.

What makes a good Quran teacher

  • Reliable recitation: solid tajweed, ideally with an ijazah or recognised training.
  • Child-friendly teaching: patience, encouragement and lively sessions.
  • Individual follow-up: listening to each child recite and correcting pronunciation.
  • Contact with parents: knowing what was learned and how to revise at home.

Reading, tajweed or memorisation: where to start?

StageFor whomWhat is learned
Reading (Noorani Qaida)Beginners from age 5–6Letters, vowels, fluent reading
TajweedChildren who already readRecitation rules and correct pronunciation
Memorisation (hifz)All levels, in parallelShort surahs first, then a progressive plan

A pace suited to age

  • Ages 5 to 7: 20 to 30 minutes, 2 to 3 times a week, with plenty of oral repetition.
  • Ages 8 to 12: 30 to 45 minutes, 3 times a week, plus 10 minutes of daily revision.
  • Teenagers: more ambitious memorisation goals with a written plan.

Whether you live in Tunisia, France, Saudi Arabia, Qatar or Canada, sessions are scheduled in your local time: late afternoon in the Gulf, Wednesday or weekends in France, Saturday and Sunday mornings in Canada.

A realistic goal for Ramadan

For a child starting in January, a reasonable goal is to master the alphabet and read simple words, or, if they already read, to memorise a few short surahs solidly with correct pronunciation. Five well-recited surahs are better than twenty approximate ones.

Frequently asked questions

At what age can a child start learning the Quran?

From 4 or 5 for listening and repetition, and around 5–6 for learning the letters and reading.

Are online Quran lessons effective for children?

Yes, provided sessions are live and one-to-one or in very small groups, so the teacher hears and corrects every child.

My child does not speak Arabic. Can they still learn the Quran?

Yes. Reading and memorisation can be learned without speaking Arabic, and learning the language alongside will later help with understanding the meaning.
