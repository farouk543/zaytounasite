Is online tutoring as effective?

Yes, on one condition: it must be live, with a teacher who interacts, asks questions and corrects in real time. Watching pre-recorded videos alone does not have the same effect. In a one-to-one or small-group online lesson, the teacher sees the student’s work on screen, spots mistakes immediately and adapts the explanation.

What makes a lesson effective is not the place. It is the quality of the teacher, the regularity of sessions and the follow-up between lessons.

From what age?

  • From 6–7 years old: short sessions (30 to 45 minutes), with a parent nearby at first.
  • Middle school: ideal to rebuild method and written work.
  • High school: especially suited to exam preparation, with past papers corrected together.

Preparing for an online lesson

  1. A quiet place with a table and good light.
  2. A computer or tablet, a stable connection and ideally a headset.
  3. Notebook and pencil case within reach, just like in class.
  4. Phone out of the way to avoid notifications.

Frequently asked questions

What equipment is needed for an online lesson?

A computer or tablet (or a smartphone if needed), a stable internet connection and a quiet place. A headset with a microphone makes a real difference.

How do I know my child is making progress?

Set a measurable goal from the start (a grade, a mastered chapter, an exam) and ask for regular check-ins with the teacher.

Can online and home tutoring be combined?

Of course. Some families keep a local tutor for one subject and choose an online specialist for another.
